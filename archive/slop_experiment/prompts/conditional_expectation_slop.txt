My dataset covers about 900 mid-sized manufacturing firms observed annually over 12 years, so it's a reasonably long and mostly balanced panel. The outcome variable is log labor productivity, and the regressors include log capital intensity, a firm-level R&D intensity ratio, an index of export exposure, and a set of year dummies to soak up aggregate shocks like recessions and commodity price swings. I'm deciding between pooled OLS, random effects, and fixed effects, and I'll probably run a Hausman test to help decide, though I know that test has its own issues when the errors are heteroskedastic or serially correlated. I'm also planning to cluster standard errors at the firm level rather than using heteroskedasticity-robust-only standard errors, since with 12 years per firm there's a real risk of serial correlation in the residuals within a firm biasing the SEs downward. Separately, I'm still deciding whether to winsorize the productivity variable at the 1st and 99th percentiles to deal with a handful of extreme outliers in the raw data, and whether to include industry-by-year fixed effects on top of the firm and year effects, since some of the productivity swings look like they're driven by industry-wide demand shocks rather than anything firm-specific.

A key assumption for panel data to ensure unbiasedness of coefficient estimates is that the conditional expectation of the idiosyncratic error given the other regressors and the time-invariant unobserved error is 0. Before I introduce you to the specific design and we brainstorm whether the assumption is true, lets consider the stronger assumption that the idiosyncratic error given the other regressors, but excluding conditioning on the time-invariant unobserved error, is 0. This assumption omits the time-invariant unobserved error and is therefore easier to discuss. Lets consider the following case: we decompose the idiosyncratic error into many parts, and some of these parts have dependence with the regressors, however in aggregate there is no dependence between the regressors and the idiosyncratic errors as these dependencies perfectly cancel out. In such a case does the assumption of expectation conditional on regressors hold true?
